Five sessions, but recovery is built into the middle and the end of the week. Each muscle group gets trained roughly twice.

Swapping exercises

Every card is a slot — a job that needs doing, like "primary chest press" or "hamstring curl" — not a fixed exercise. The swap arrows on each card pull a different movement that does the same job with the equipment you have. Shuffle day re-rolls the whole session.

Swapping a card that already has numbers in it clears that card for the week, since those numbers belonged to a different lift. Tap the arrows twice to confirm.

Turn on Auto-vary under Gear and each new week rolls a fresh selection on its own. Leave it off and your picks carry forward unchanged, which makes progression easier to read.

You do not need to train to failure to rebuild muscle. Strength comes back fast because of muscle memory — tendons and connective tissue do not. Don't let a quick jump in strength talk you into loading the bar every week.

Double progression

Stay at one weight until every set hits the top of the rep range, then add weight and start at the bottom again.

  • Week 1 — 135 × 8, 8, 7
  • Week 2 — 135 × 9, 9, 8
  • Week 3 — 135 × 10, 10, 10 → move up
  • Week 4 — 145 × 7, 7, 6

Each set row shows what you logged the week before — and names the lift, so a swapped exercise never reads as a strength drop.

Rest between sets

  • 2–3 min — presses, rows, squats, RDLs, leg press.
  • 60–90 sec — curls, extensions, lateral raises, calves.

Don't cut rest short to make the session feel harder. This is resistance training, not cardio.

Kettlebell notes

Kettlebells log the same way as anything else — enter the bell weight. For double kettlebell work, log the weight of one bell and stay consistent week to week. Swings are the one exercise here that lives in the 12–20 rep range on purpose: it's a hinge you drive with your hips, not a squat, and not a shoulder raise.

Session length

Keep the first month to 50–70 minutes. Leave feeling like you trained hard enough to grow — and could have done a little more.
